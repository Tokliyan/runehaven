## The two run4 failures, and the repair

Both were SOURCE-TEXT gates, not pictures. `S2 R3` extracts every
`const [ax, ay] = worldToScreen(...)` argument list out of the foam block's
text and requires the set to be exactly the four insets; `S2 R4` requires
four literal `worldToScreen(...)` strings. The S4 tide patch had folded the
swash into those arguments (`ty + 0.08 - td`), so both stopped matching
although the picture was right.

Fixed in the code, as the slice required: the four `worldToScreen` calls are
byte for byte what `main` has again, and the swash is added to each stroke's
two SCREEN points instead. The shift is per edge, which is why it cannot be
one translate for the block — world `(0, -td)` is `(+td·IW2, -td·IH2)` on
screen and world `(-td, 0)` is `(-td·IW2, -td·IH2)`: same y, mirrored x. It
adds no canvas call. **Correction to the slice's own text:** this block is
not in `drawGroundTile`; it is in `render()`'s water/lava animation loop
(runehaven.html:25208, inside `function render(t)` at 24993). That matters,
because a `ctx.translate` there — the slice's suggested fix — would have
been the wrong instrument for a different reason than the one considered:
not baking into ground-cache chunks (this block is live), but costing three
canvas calls a stroke. The screen-point form costs none.

Equivalence: exact in the shipped configuration. With `coast: false`,
`pbAirTide` returns 0, so every offset is `0`/`-0` and the coordinates are
bit-identical to before. With the coast switch on, the two forms differ only
by double rounding — measured worst case **7.3e-12 px** over 960
tile-and-phase probes (an independent reviewer measured 1.46e-11 px over
200k probes with the camera 90k px from the origin). Nothing rasterises
differently at that scale.

## The 14 new run4 gates and the 2 new run6 gates

**Wind** (the field itself; the clock is pinned with `debugSetDayT(null,
epoch)` for the whole block, because `WIND` reads `tS = ms/1000 -
worldEpoch` and `worldEpoch` comes from the world row's `created_at` — an
un-pinned wind gate measures a different wind on every run, which is what
made the first draft's figures move).

1. **One deterministic field.** 300 probes repeat exactly; 17 ms always
   moves the lean; the direction is hashed off `worldSeed`; nothing in
   `windFrame`/`windGust`/`windLean`/`pbLeanFrame`/`windAmpNow` reads a
   clock, a random or a per-object hash.
2. **Bounds.** 2000 probes across all five weather kinds: unit direction
   (error 2.2e-16), |sx| 0.450–0.801 and never under 0.4, strength always
   inside 0.64×–1× its own weather, gust inside 0–1, |lean| up to 0.706
   under the 1.254 the formula allows, lean frame always a whole −2..2. The
   |sx| ceiling is derived from the source's own `0.70711` — a shade over
   1/√2, so |sx| reaches a hair past 1 — rather than rounded to 1, or the
   gate would fail on the field's own arithmetic.
3. **Off is nothing moving.** Lean and frame are 0 at every probe at Off;
   Low is exactly half of Full; Reduce motion forces amp 0; all 8 readers of
   the gust scale it by `WIND.amp` or bail without it.
4. **One band, crossing in order.** Measured as the band, not two lone
   points: the gust profile along the wind keeps its shape and moves
   downwind by exactly `WIND_GUST_SPEED` tiles a second (0.003–0.014
   residual against 0.16–0.26 unmoved), its crests 11 tiles apart by
   autocorrelation (r ≥ 0.986). A tree leans the same way as the grass at
   its feet and never further.

**Surf** (5). The tide is the only thing moved onto the foam's screen
points, and it moves them exactly where the world-space form put them. See
"the repair's own gate" below.

**Forge** (6–8). Stable keys: 120 Plains tuft and tree keys, all distinct,
byte-identical two frames and a moved camera later, a lily pad's five lean
frames collapsing to one key, and no key line reading a clock, a frame
counter, a camera or a random. The colour cap: the quantiser takes 256
distinct colours to exactly 40, `pbBake` runs it before and after the
finish, and the Plains ground texture — real art this harness can read,
since `pbTexGen` builds its 192×192 tileable buffer with no canvas — is 12
colours and byte-identical twice. The cache budget: 2460 added sprites hold
the shared cache at 2400, eight 4M-pixel sprites hold it at the 16M-pixel
budget, and the scenery forge's own per-frame budget (4 bakes / 4 ms, trees
3 / 6 ms, props 40) is reset by `pbFrameStart`.

**Life** (9–11). jsdom CAN run the live path once the three bake entry
points are stood in for (the first failed bake would otherwise switch the
forge off and the module draws nothing without it). Determinism: a cell's
plan is byte-identical when rebuilt, unchanged with the viewer 900 tiles
away, different in the next window, and none of the 20 plan and step
functions reads a random, a clock, the camera or the player; live, the same
instant replays the same 36 creatures after every plan is thrown away. The
cap: over 24 coastal camera-and-hour probes on the pinned harness seed the
drawn count is always `min(candidates, 24)`; the densest cell raises 36 and
draws exactly 24, in `d2` order, nothing beyond 23.3 tiles surviving the
cull. Not targetable: six real swings taken standing on live creatures leave
the mob list and the player's hp untouched with nothing in weapon reach; a
creature record carries 16 drawing fields and no hp, death, damage or
target; the module never names `mobs`, `others`, `wilds`, `critters`, `hp`,
`damage`, `hurt` or `tamed`; `"pblife"` reaches the entity list for drawing
only.

**Perf** (12–14). Frame cost, measured twice because both answers matter:
in the steady state the budget describes, a Plains screen with the biome ON
takes **3510** canvas operations a frame against **10428** with it OFF
(×0.337 — baked scenery is *cheaper* than the live art it replaces); in the
worst case, the forge off and every tree falling back to live drawing,
**11213** against **10829** (×1.035). Both inside the 1.15× limit. Off is a
still frame: the 588 scenery sprites a Plains frame asks for are the same
588 at world times 1 s, 5 s and 20 s apart, while at Full the same gaps move
50, 61 and 345 of them. And the wind allocates nothing a frame (`windFrame`
hands back the one `WIND` object; no allocating syntax in the three
functions) while `pbSprite` keeps its own budget, back-off and off-switch
and never writes the heroes'.

**run6** (real browser, where a bake can be read back). 216 Plains tuft,
tree, ground-prop and decor sprites bake into alpha 0/255 only, at most 40
colours (most used is exactly 40, so the quantiser is doing the work), never
empty, 0 null. And the 6 ms budget: those 216 bakes average **0.60–0.67 ms**
(p95 1.40–1.60) and a whole 16×16-tile ground chunk with Plains on bakes in
**1.26–2.49 ms** on average, 1.70–3.00 ms at worst, over nine warm chunks.
The gate is on the warm figure, and it held on every run.

**Worth the owner's eye:** the FIRST, cold chunk — which also bakes whatever
ground props it meets, outside the per-frame budget, up to
`PB_FORCE_PER_FRAME` of them — measured 5.90 ms on an idle machine and
**9.20 ms** on the final run after four harnesses had been through. So a
cold Plains chunk can cross the 6 ms budget under load. It is reported
rather than gated, because gating a figure that moves 60% with machine load
would be gating the machine; and because the honest fix is a decision —
either the cold chunk is allowed its prop bakes (the current design, and the
cost is paid once per chunk) or `PB_FORCE_PER_FRAME` comes down and props
appear a frame late. That is a Plains look-and-feel decision for slice 1b or
the owner, not something to pick at 5am. The cache ends at 210 sprites and
0.25M pixels.

### Where each gate lives, and why

Everything that needs a readable canvas is in run6; everything else is in
run4. In jsdom the scenery forge switches itself off on its first failed
bake (`pbSprite` sets `pbForgeOk` false; the hero forge sets `FORGE_ON`
false on its own), so a colour count or a bake time measured there would be
a number about a stub. The 1.15× frame cost is in run4 on purpose: it is
counted in canvas operations, which is deterministic, where a real frame
*rate* on a loaded machine is not — and the frame rate already has run6
gates of its own.

   gates match the stroke TEXT; after the repair the drawn point is

   suggested `ctx.translate` as well. **Fixed by gate 5, new tonight:** it
   pins the four `moveTo`/`lineTo` lines and the two lines that define the

## Judgment calls this slice

1. **Where the gates live.** The slice says the gates were missing from
   "run4/run5/run6" without saying which. Everything needing a readable
   canvas went to run6 and everything else to run4, for the reason in
   "Where each gate lives". run5 is a coverage sweep, not a gate file, and
   gained nothing; no new species, mob, weapon kind or class was added
   tonight, so README step 7 had nothing to extend.
2. **The 1.15× frame cost is counted in canvas operations,** not frames per
   second — deterministic, where a frame rate on a loaded machine is not,
   and reported both baked and worst-case so neither figure flatters the
   other.
3. **The life gates are exercised on a coastal cell.** The seven implemented
   ambient kinds have no Plains habitat (Plains's rabbits, crows and
   butterflies are the older ambient layer, not this module), so a
   Plains-only life gate would be vacuous. The RULES gated are the module's
   — one cap for every kind together, one plan per cell and window, nothing
   you can hit — and `sand`/`coast` are switched on only for the duration
   and put back. Plains is left false. I did not gate "Plains draws no
   ambient creature": true today, but a future slice adding Plains rabbits
   to this module would have to loosen it, and gates are not supposed to
   need loosening.
4. **Mutation checks were run per gate group** as each was written, rather
   than all at the end as the slice's sentence order implies. The end state
   is identical and each pushed step was complete and verified on its own,
   which the slice's own rule 6 (the usage limit has killed a night before)
   argues for.
5. **The clock, the seed and the three bake entry points are pinned or
   stood in for** inside the gates, and all handed back in a `finally`. Any
   figure quoted by a gate would otherwise move between runs.
